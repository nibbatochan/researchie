# What science and medicine thesis students need: evidence for the one-line USP

Date: 2026-10-17. Target user: master's, PhD and medical/health-science students writing a thesis. Competitors: Zotero, Notion, NotebookLM, Obsidian.

How to read the labels. **PRIMARY** = I opened the study, paper, vendor page or forum thread (or the search tool returned its abstract). **ANECDOTAL** = forum post, one person's words. **UNVERIFIED** = only a secondary source (news, blog, search snippet of a page I could not open); treat as a lead. Every number has a source link, a sample size and a year next to it.

---

## 1. Summary in plain language (10 lines)

1. Students now use AI for reading and summarising, but most do not trust it. In a 2025 survey of about 3,800 PhD students, about four in five said they do not fully trust AI tools (UNVERIFIED figure, from a Nature news feature I could not open).
2. AI invents references. In controlled tests, 18% to 91% of AI-written citations were fake, depending on model and year. Real published papers also misquote their sources: about 1 in 4 citations in medical journals is a quotation error.
3. So the strongest, best-evidenced need is: "I can trust this quote and this citation, and I can prove it." Researchie already builds this (verified quotes, verified DOI, text-fragment links).
4. Retracted papers keep getting cited. Only about 5% of citations made after a retraction mention it (7,813 retracted papers, 2021 study). In 74% of systematic reviews that included a retracted study, the retraction came after the review was published (50 reviews, 2025). A "watch" that tells you later has real value.
5. Bad news for "retraction alerts" as the headline: Zotero has done this for free since 2019, and now includes expressions of concern. Alone it will not make anyone switch.
6. Universities are starting to require a written declaration of AI use in the thesis (UCL, Oxford department, Monash, Open University, DCU). Nobody gives students a log that makes that declaration easy. This is a real, checkable, thesis-specific gap.
7. The idea of a question-first literature matrix has almost no direct evidence behind it. Library guides recommend matrices, but I found no study showing students want a tool for it. Test before building.
8. I found no study measuring what students would pay for a reference tool. The only hard price facts: Zotero is free (300 MB storage, 2 GB for $20/year), EndNote student licence is $150 one-off, NotebookLM free allows 50 sources per notebook. Students expect free tools from their university.
9. Verdict: the current position ("trace every claim to its source and watch the sources") is supported, but "watch the sources" is not unique and "trace every claim" is the stronger half. The honest USP is trust in what you write, with source health as proof, not as the headline.
10. Do not decide from this file alone. Run the interview guide and survey in section 9 with 5 to 10 students first.

## 3. The real thesis workflow

**Evidence limit up front.** I found no large study that follows students through all ten steps. The workflow below is assembled from smaller studies and official documents. Treat step order as typical, not measured. The step-level pain points in section 4 carry the evidence labels.

| Step | What students typically do | Tools | Where information is lost or retyped by hand | Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Search | PubMed, Google Scholar, library databases, now AI chat | PubMed, Scholar, Web of Science, ChatGPT | Search strings and "why I rejected this" are not saved anywhere shared with the library | AI use for literature tasks is the most accepted AI use among PhD students: [Angelini and Lyrvall 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25678) (preprint, uses Nature Graduate Survey 2025, n=3,785 STEM and health PhD students). UNVERIFIED for percentages (PDF would not open). |
| Collect | Save PDFs and web pages into a reference manager | Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley | A web page becomes a snapshot or bookmark, not a located passage | Reference-manager adoption is uneven: 545 postgraduates in Greece, about two-thirds non-users, many unaware the library recommended a tool ([Greece study, abstract only](https://lekythos.library.ucy.ac.cy/handle/10797/26916)); 81 Australian postgraduates, 71.4% used one ([abstract](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/RSR-02-2013-0007/full/html), 2013 era, sample skewed to trained users). |
| Read and annotate | Highlight in a PDF reader, margin notes | Zotero reader, Adobe, GoodNotes, Notion (embedded PDF) | Highlights are stored inside the tool; other programs cannot read Zotero annotations ([Zotero forum, ANECDOTAL](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/403900)) | ANECDOTAL |
| Note | Summaries and "key finding" notes | Word, OneNote, Notion, Obsidian | The note often loses the page number and the exact words | Quotation error rate 25.4% in medical literature (section 4, row 1) |
| Organise | Folders, tags, a table of papers (a "literature matrix" in Excel/Word) | Excel, Notion databases, Zotero collections | Re-typing author, year, method, finding for each paper | Library guides recommend matrices ([TUS](https://tus.ie/app/uploads/CampusLife/Access/Midlands/Literature-Review-Matrix-1.docx), [JCU](https://www.jcu.edu.au/students/learningcentre/orientation-for-intensive-courses/Using-a-Reading-Matrix.pdf), practitioner guides, not studies) |
| Cite | Insert citations while writing | Zotero/EndNote/Mendeley Word plug-ins; BibTeX in LaTeX | Fields corrupt in long documents ([Zotero forum, ANECDOTAL](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/278241/)) | ANECDOTAL |
| Write | Draft chapters; AI used for rewriting and structure | Word (medicine, health sciences), LaTeX/Overleaf (physics, maths, CS, engineering), Google Docs | Citations and quotes are typed or pasted again from notes | Discipline pattern: Word dominant in medicine and life sciences, LaTeX in maths, physics, CS, engineering ([Knauff and Nejasmic, PLOS ONE 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4272305/), n=40 experiment; discipline pattern is the authors' description, not a measured split) |
| Submit | University template, declarations, AI-use statement | University portal | AI-use statement written from memory | UCL, Oxford, Monash, OU, DCU policies (section 6) |
| Viva | Re-defend claims and sources | Printed thesis, notes | Hard to find where a claim came from | No study found |

**Medicine vs sciences vs system.** Differences I can support: citation style (Vancouver/AMA in medicine; many sciences use numbered or author-date styles) and word processor (above). I could not find a source that compares how medical and science PhD students differ in *collecting or reading* tools. State it as unknown.

| Do researchers *want* alerts? | No survey found | The only evidence is behavioural: Zotero built it and kept it ([Zotero beta thread, 7 June 2019](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/333554), 12 comments, shipped in 5.0.67 on 14 June 2019). | ANECDOTAL |

How Zotero's alert was received (all ANECDOTAL, forum):
- Beta thread, 12 comments: mostly requests for a "View" button and a Retracted Items collection; one user "simply praised" it. Dev added both within days.
- Limits stated by Zotero: works only for items with a DOI or PMID, about three quarters of Retraction Watch data ([thread](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/333554)).
- Data later moved to Crossref and now includes expressions of concern ([blog](https://www.zotero.org/blog/retracted-item-notification/), search summary).
- False positives and flicker reported: [Paper incorrectly flagged as retracted](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/109653/paper-incorrectly-flagged-as-retracted), [Correcting a Retraction Watch error](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/130991/correcting-a-retractiona-watch-error). The Zotero 8 release note does not mention retraction ([blog](https://www.zotero.org/blog/zotero-8/)).

What this means for the product. The evidence says retractions go unnoticed and that late retractions matter in medicine. It does not say students are asking for alerts, and the biggest competitor already ships them. The remaining gap is: (a) sources that are not DOI/PMID items (web pages, preprints); (b) "page changed or gone"; (c) following the flag into every note, answer and exported claim; (d) a one-page "source health" report for the thesis. Evidence for (c) and (d) as *wanted* is currently zero, only plausible.

## 6. AI use, distrust, and institutional rules

**What students use AI for**
- HEPI (UK, undergraduates only, so weak for PhD): 88% used generative AI for assessments in 2025, up from 53%; 18% put AI text directly in work; top uses explaining concepts, summarising articles, suggesting research ideas ([HEPI 2025](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/2025/02/26/student-generative-ai-survey-2025/), n=1,041 full-time undergraduates, Savanta, 2025). 2026 edition: 94% use generative AI for assessed work, n=1,054 ([HEPI 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/), December 2025).
- PhD students (STEM and health): accept AI for tracking and summarising literature, resist it for writing papers, data analysis and experiment design ([Angelini and Lyrvall 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2608.25678.html), preprint, n=3,785 Nature Graduate Survey 2025; four attitude groups; preprint, unreviewed).
- Thesis writers (MBA, small): 88.6% of AI users used it to understand and organise prior literature; 78.5% for drafting or rewriting ([arXiv 2604.02792](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02792v1), n=83, 2026).
- Researchers overall: AI use 57% to 84% from 2024 to 2025; 57% say lack of guidelines and training is a main obstacle ([Wiley, October 2025 press release](https://newsroom.wiley.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2025/AI-Adoption-Jumps-to-84-Among-Researchers-as-Expectations-Undergo-Significant-Reality-Check/), n=2,430, August 2025; vendor survey). Elsevier: 58% use AI, up from 37% (3,000+ researchers, 2025).
- Disclosure gap: about a third of 1,017 researchers never disclosed AI use when submitting; in medicine 70% always disclose, STEM 34% ([Springer Nature, April 2026](https://www.springernature.com/gp/advancing-discovery/springboard/blog/blogposts-technology-partnerships/researchers-embrace-ai-the-disclosure-gap-is-the-challenge-/52326544), published 2026, fieldwork 2025).

**What they distrust**: invented references (section 4, row 2), shallow or agreeable answers ("overly agreeable" in interviews, arXiv 2604.02792), loss of own thinking (21.5% in that study; 65% in Nature survey, UNVERIFIED).

**What they would trust**: visible citations and sources (59% in Elsevier survey above); human checking (49%, Elsevier, via search summary, UNVERIFIED). No study tests whether *verifiable quotes* specifically raise trust; that is an inference from "transparency and clear citations" ranking first.

**University policies (primary documents, fetched by search; check current wording before quoting)**
- UCL: declare generative AI use in doctoral work including the viva; student is responsible for accuracy; acceptable use varies by discipline ([UCL Doctoral School](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/study/doctoral-school/regulations/essential-procedures-and-policies/transparency-authorship-and-generative-ai-doctoral-research)).
- Oxford (Materials department page only, not a university-wide policy): AI-use statement after the abstract; list each tool's name, version, publisher and each use case ([Oxford Materials](https://materialsoutreach.web.ox.ac.uk/node/4935751)).
- Monash: thesis submission includes online questions on generative AI use; preliminary pages prompt acknowledgement ([Monash graduate research](https://www.monash.edu/graduate-research/support-and-resources/resources/guidance-on-generative-ai)).
- Open University: declarations expected at all stages of doctoral research ([OU position statement](https://oro.open.ac.uk/105483)).
- Dublin City University: tools used and their purpose must be listed in the thesis ([DCU guidance, September 2026](https://www.dcu.ie/sites/default/files/registry_access/2026-09/guidance-for-doctoral-students-and-supervisors-on-reporting-use-of-gen-ai_september_2026.pdf)).
- Pattern: **declare tool, version, purpose, and take responsibility for accuracy.** None of these gives students a tool to produce that record. That is the base for USP 2.

What this implies: the default expectation is "free via the library". A paid product must either beat free on something students will not do by hand, or be free for students and paid by someone else (a lab, a university, a publisher). Switching cost is mainly *migrating a library of hundreds of references* and *risk to a half-written thesis*. Evidence for that cost is the Zotero Word-field threads (ANECDOTAL), not a study. Practical consequence: Zotero import and Word export must exist before any student switches mid-thesis.

## 8. Competitor reality check (official sources and forums)

Vote counts: the Zotero forum does not show votes; I give reply counts where I could read them. Obsidian shows likes. For Notion and NotebookLM I could not retrieve a feedback forum with counts; I say so.

### Zotero
- **What a thesis student does with it**: collects papers, annotates PDFs, inserts citations in Word/Google Docs/LibreOffice, free.
- **Official**: Zotero 8 puts annotations (PDF, EPUB, webpage snapshots) under their parent items, searchable, taggable; new unified citation dialog; notes open in tabs ([Zotero 8](https://www.zotero.org/blog/zotero-8/)). Retraction flags for DOI/PMID items ([2019 thread](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/333554)).
- **Not in the official post** (absent from the release note, which is not the same as "cannot do"): AI features, retraction checking.
- **Top missing-feature requests found (ANECDOTAL)**:
  - AI help with reading: [thread started 14 Jan 2023](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/102320/the-possibility-of-integrating-ai-into-zotero-to-assist-reading), at least 30 replies on page 1, no staff reply seen; first reply: it "would depend entirely on whether those tools have APIs".
  - AI chat across the library with citations to paper and page: [BYOK request, 22 June 2026](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/514500/), 2 replies, no staff reply. A moderator in a similar thread pointed to plug-ins ([thread](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/509364)).
  - Read/unread flag: [2026 thread](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/132442/feature-request-first-class-read-unread-for-papers) and [Mark as Read](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/25165).
  - Corrupted Word citation fields: several threads (section 4, row 6).

### NotebookLM
- **Thesis use**: upload PDFs and notes, ask questions, get cited answers.
- **Official limits**: 50 sources on the free plan; 500,000 words or 200 MB per source; copy-protected PDFs fail; short sources may be cited as the whole document, not a passage ([Google support](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16269187)). The page lists no notebook export and no scholarly citation format. (Absence on that page, not proof it cannot.)
- **Missing-feature requests with counts**: not retrieved. UNVERIFIED claims in the earlier competitor file (source blindness, text-only web import) still need a primary source.

### Notion
- **Thesis use**: databases for a paper tracker, notes, collaboration.
- **Official**: Web Clipper clips whole pages; mobile clips "entire pages"; no tags during clipping; some content may not clip; the help page does not mention partial pages, selected text or highlights ([Notion help](https://www.notion.com/help/web-clipper)).
- **PDF annotation**: no official page found. Third-party guides disagree: UNVERIFIED.
- **Requests with counts**: not retrieved.

### Obsidian
- **Thesis use**: linked Markdown notes, Zotero via plug-ins.
- **Official**: Web Clipper supports a highlighter, templates and selection capture ([Obsidian help](https://help.obsidian.md/web-clipper/highlight)).
- **Requests (ANECDOTAL)**: native PDF highlighting/annotation, [thread started 21 Jan 2022](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/highlighting-annotation-of-pdfs-as-a-core-feature-of-obsidian-annotate/31015): at least 20 replies, first post 13 likes (search summary); staff reply: "Developer hours aren't infinite, so knowing more about why this matters to you will help with prioritization." (EleanorKonik, 21 Jan 2022). Zotero link: [Add Logseq-Zotero-functionality](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/add-logseq-zotero-functionality/29523), 5 likes. Plug-in dependence and breakage: [PDF-Zotero-Obsidian thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/pdf-zotero-obsidian-current-state-and-collaboration-for-the-one-plugin/34372).

## 10. Interview guide (10 questions, 30 minutes, 5 to 10 students)

Ask for the last real thing they did, not what they would do. Do not mention Researchie until question 10.

1. Walk me through the last time you added a source to your thesis, from finding it to having it in your draft. Which tools, which steps did you retype?
2. Show me one citation in your draft. Where did it come from? How sure are you the claim is in that source?
3. Tell me about the last time you could not find where a quote or fact came from. What did you do? How long did it take?
4. Have you ever cited a paper and later found it was corrected or retracted? How did you find out? What if you had found out after submission?
5. How do you keep track of what you have read, are reading, and rejected? Where does that live?
6. Do you use AI for your thesis? Show me the last time. What did you do with the answer before you used it?
7. Has AI ever given you a source or quote that was wrong? What happened?
8. Does your university or supervisor require a statement about AI use? How would you write it today, and how long would it take?
9. What do you pay for today, and what do you refuse to pay for? What would a tool need to do for you to pay, or ask your lab or university to pay?
10. (Now show one screen: a clip with "verified quote" and "source health". Do not explain.) What do you think this does? What would you do next? Would you use it for a chapter this month, and what stops you?

Scoring: for each of the top 2 USPs, count students who (a) described a recent real incident without prompting, (b) currently spend time on a workaround, (c) said they would try it this month. Keep the USP if 4 of 7 say (a) and (b); drop it if fewer than 2.

## 11. Survey (8 questions, 3 minutes)

1. Stage: master's / PhD / medical or health science / other; year of study.
2. Which do you use for references? (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley, none, other). Which word processor for your thesis? (Word, Google Docs, LaTeX/Overleaf, other)
3. In the last 3 months, how often have you been unable to find where a quote or fact in your notes came from? (never / once / 2 to 5 times / more than 5 times)
4. How often do you use AI for thesis work? For what? (search, summarise, explain, rewrite, citations, other) How often do you check its sources by hand? (never / sometimes / always)
5. Has AI given you a reference or quote that did not exist or was wrong? (never / once / several times)
6. Does your university require you to declare AI use in the thesis? How hard would it be to list it? (1 to 5)
7. Rate how much you would value each (1 to 5): (a) a check that every quote and citation matches its source; (b) a log of AI use for your thesis declaration; (c) alerts when a source you cited is retracted, corrected or changes; (d) a table that sorts papers by research question; (e) one-click export to Word/LaTeX with working references.
8. What would you pay per month, if anything, for the one you rated highest? (0 / up to 5 / 5 to 10 / 10 to 20 / more) Who would pay: you, your lab, your university?

Decision rule: a USP is confirmed if at least 60% rate it 4 or 5 *and* at least a third would pay or ask someone to pay. With only 5 to 10 respondents, treat this as direction, not proof.

## 12. Limits of this research

- Several searches returned abstracts and snippets only. Where I could not open the paper I marked it UNVERIFIED or "abstract via search".
- The 2025 Nature Graduate Survey percentages (81% distrust, 65% skill worry) come from a Nature feature whose PDF would not open and a Springer Nature community profile; the sample (3,785) and design are confirmed by the data record and an arXiv preprint.
- I found no Ithaka S+R study on graduate students and reference tools; no survey on retraction-alert demand; no study on willingness to pay for research tools; no study on supervisor-feedback tooling.
- HEPI figures are for UK undergraduates, not thesis students.
- AI fabrication rates date from 2023 to mid-2025 models; they are not a statement about current retrieval-based tools.
- Vendor surveys (Wiley, Elsevier, Springer Nature) have commercial interests.
