# Student interview sheet (30 minutes)

Goal: learn what really happens when a student writes a thesis, so we can pick what to build. You are not selling anything. Aim for 5 to 10 science or medicine students who are writing a thesis now or finished in the last year.

## Before you start (2 minutes)

Say this: "I'm building a tool for students writing a thesis. I want to hear how you really work, including the messy parts. There are no right answers and I'm not testing you. Can I take notes? I won't use your name."

- Take notes by hand or on your phone. Do not record unless they agree.
- Do not mention Researchie until question 10.
- Always ask for **the last real time**, not "what would you do". Follow up with "show me" or "what happened next?".

## Questions

1. Walk me through the last time you added a source to your thesis, from finding it to having it in your draft. Which tools did you use? Which steps did you type or copy by hand?
2. Open one citation in your draft. Where did it come from? How sure are you the claim is in that source?
3. Tell me about the last time you could not find where a quote or fact came from. What did you do? How long did it take?
4. Have you ever cited a paper and then found out it was corrected or retracted? How did you find out? What would it mean if you found out after submitting?
5. How do you keep track of what you have read, are reading, and rejected? Where does that live?
6. Do you use AI for your thesis? Show me the last time. What did you do with the answer before using it?
7. Has AI ever given you a source or quote that was wrong or did not exist? What happened?
8. Does your university or supervisor ask you to declare AI use in the thesis? How would you write that today, and how long would it take?
9. What do you pay for today for studying or writing, and what do you refuse to pay for? What would a tool have to do for you to pay, or to ask your lab or university to pay?
10. (Only now. Show one screen of Researchie, a saved quote with its verified source. Do not explain it.) What do you think this does? What would you do next? Would you use it for a chapter this month? What stops you?

## Scoring (after 5 or more interviews)

For each idea, count the students who (a) described a **recent real incident** without being pushed and (b) currently spend time on a **workaround**.

| Idea | Question to count | Keep if | Drop if |
| :-- | :-- | :-: | :-: |
| Every quote and citation checked against its source | Q2, Q3, Q7 | 4 of 7 say (a) and (b) | fewer than 2 |
| Alerts when a cited paper is retracted or corrected | Q4 | 4 of 7 say (a) and (b) | fewer than 2 |
| AI-use record for the declaration | Q6, Q8 | 4 of 7 say (a) and (b) | fewer than 2 |
| Pay or ask someone to pay | Q9 | at least 1 in 3 | none |

With 5 to 10 people this shows direction, not proof. Write down surprises: anything they hate or spend time on that we have not listed matters more than the ideas above.

## Optional 3-minute survey (send to classmates and labs)

1. Stage (master's, PhD, medical or health science, other) and year.
2. Reference tool (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley, none, other) and word processor (Word, Google Docs, LaTeX or Overleaf, other).
3. In the last 3 months, how often could you not find where a quote or fact in your notes came from? (never, once, 2 to 5 times, more than 5 times)
4. How often do you use AI for thesis work, and how often do you check its sources by hand? (never, sometimes, always)
5. Has AI given you a reference or quote that did not exist or was wrong? (never, once, several times)
6. Does your university require you to declare AI use in the thesis? If yes, how hard would it be to list it? (1 to 5)
7. Rate 1 to 5 how useful each would be: (a) a check that every quote and citation matches its source; (b) a log of AI use for your declaration; (c) alerts when a cited paper is retracted, corrected or changes; (d) a table that sorts papers by research question; (e) export to Word or LaTeX with working references.
8. What would you pay per month for the one you rated highest? (0, up to 5, 5 to 10, 10 to 20, more) Who would pay: you, your lab, your university?
